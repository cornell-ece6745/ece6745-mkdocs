    Final Report

    Each group must submit a final report which documents your chip testing efforts throughout the semester. Please format your document with page numbers, single-spacing, 10-point font, 1" margins. You must use Times New Roman or Palatino font. Clearly mark each section with a numbered section header. All diagrams, tables, and figures should be numbered with a caption and be included in the main narrative of the text as opposed to placed at the end of the document. You must include the sections below. Please clearly number and label all sections. You might want to have a bold "paragraph title" at the beginning of each paragraph (e.g., "Design:", "Pre-Silicon Verification", etc) as shown below. Make sure your figures, diagrams, and photos are all well formatted. You need to carefully size them so they are not so big that there are pages and pages of figures, diagrams, and photos but also not so small that you cannot see anything. Consider carefully grouping several figures, diagrams, and photos onto a single page to help structure the report. All reports must be submitted on Canvas in PDF format.

    The final report should four sections:

    • Section 1: Introduction (one paragraph)
    • Section 2: Tiny Flow Tape-Out (five paragraphs)
    • Section 3: Accelerator Tape-Out (five paragraphs)
    • Section 4: Conclusion (one paragraph)

    This handout tells you exactly what to focus on for each paragraph. You can make it a little longer if you like, or have more paragraphs if you like, but this handout explains the minimum expectations.

    Section 1: Introduction

    • The introduction should be 1 paragraph and should briefly summarize the two chips you taped out last spring: your tiny flow tape-out and your accelerator tape-out. Mention that your tiny flow tapeout is a 100x100um block on a larger chip in TSMC 180nm, while your accelerator tapeout is your own 1x1mm chip in TSMC 180nm. Be sure to mention that your tiny flow tape-out is simple and uses your own RTL-to-GDS tiny flow, while your accelerator tape-out is more complex and uses an RTL-to-GDS commercial flow. You should also include a brief summary of the chip testing results (e.g., your block on the tiny flow tape-out was exhaustively tested and fully functional across all 256 inputs, accelerator tape-out was fully functional at the nominal voltage of 1.8V and nominal frequency of XMHz, also mention a fast operating point and a slow operating point where the accelreator was fully functional).

    Section 2: Tiny Flow Tape-Out

    • Design: Include one paragraph that summarizes your tiny flow block design. You should revisit your ECE 6745 Project 1 report. Include a figure with the Verilog for your tiny flow block design. Just include the the contents of the module not the interface. The code must fit on one page. If the code would be too small to fit on one page, then just include the most important Verilog snippets. Here is an example of the Verilog code for the ripple carry adder tiny flow block. You can also include a block diagram if that would help (i.e., a block diagram of a four-bit ripple carry adder).

    • Pre-Silicon Verification: Include one paragraph about your pre-silicon verification strategy. Here you want to discuss how you used exhaustive testing to ensure your tiny flow block was fully operational. It will probably be a short paragraph.

    • RTL-to-GDS Flow: Include one paragraph that discusses the RTL-to-GDS tiny flow you implemented last spring which enabled taking your design from Verilog to layout. Include the figure from last spring which illustrate the overall tiny flow. Include one sentence each on the algorithm you used for technology mapping, static timing analysis, placement, and routing.

    • Tape-Out: Include a figure of your final place-and-routed tiny flow block. Here is an example of what the tiny flow block looks like for the ripple carry adder. Explain how 25 tiny flow blocks were integrated into a tape-out with additional power routing and muxing logic at the top-level. Mention the tiny flow chip's pin out which includes I/O VDD, core VDD, ground, 5-bit block select input, 8-bit block data input, and 8-bit block data output. You should include the tiny flow chip plot and a zoomed in die photo of the tiny flow chip.

    • Post-Silicon Chip Testing: Include one paragraph that discusses the tiny flow chip testing platform and results. You should include a photo of the tiny flow chip testing board. You should explain that the board enables manually testing inputs and also automatically testing inputs and verifying outputs using the USB-to-GPIO adapter. Then you should mention the chip testing results (your block on the tiny flow tape-out was exhaustively tested and fully functional across all 256 inputs).

    Section 3: Accelerator Tape-Out

    • Design: Include one paragraph that summarizes your accelerator design. You should revisit your ECE 6745 Project 2 report, and you probably want to include a figure or block diagram from that report. You should also include the block diagram that shows how the accelerator is connected to the SPI minion and debug unit for the final chip design.

    • Pre-Silicon Verification: Include one paragraph about your pre-silicon verification strategy. You should revisit your ECE 6745 Project 2 report. Be sure to discuss your directed and random testing. Focus just on testing your chip as opposed to testing the processor+accelerator composition. Discuss how you were able to reuse the tests for your accelerator in isolation to test the SPI+accelerator chip. This will be a much longer paragraph than for your tiny flow tape-out.

    • RTL-to-GDS Flow: Include one paragraph that discusses the commercial RTL-to-GDS flow you used last spring which enabled taking your design from Verilog to layout. Include the figure from last spring which illustrate the overall commercial flow. Be sure to mention which commercial tools were used for two-state RTL simulation, four-state RTL simulation, synthesis, fast-functional GL simulation, place-and-route, static timing analysis, back-annotated GL simulation, power analysis, design rule checking, and layout vs. schematic.

    • Tape-Out: Include one paragraph that discusses the accelerator tape-out. Include a figure of your final place-and-routed GDS. Use the chip plots which are in the shared Google drive folder. Include a figure of the chip pin out and discuss the different pins of the chip (i.e., I/O VDD, core VDD, ground, clock in, clock out, reset, SPI, debug mode, debug out). Mention that the chip was packaged using wire bonding in a 68-pin ceramic pin-grid array (CPGA) package, and that we only used 32 of those pins. You should include the a zoomed out die photo of your chip (i.e., we should be able to see all of the bond wires) and a zoomed in die photo of your chip (i.e., we should be able to see the corner chip art). If you were able to get a good photo of metal 5 highlighted then include that photo along with the corresponding M5/M6 chip plot and discuss.

    • Post-Silicon Chip Testing: Include one paragraph that discusses the accelerator chip testing platform and results. Include the chip testing system diagram. You should include a photo of your accelerator chip testing board. You should annotate the following components on the chip testing board: accelerator chip; power LED; clock in and clock out; USB-to-SPI adapter; reset button; debug mode switch; and debug mode LED. You should explain that the board enables debug mode and explain what debug mode is and how it works. You should explain how you did the accelerator testing by using xmsgs. Then you should include a figure of your shmoo plot and summarize the region of operation where the chip is fully functional. Explain why the chip starts to fail at low voltage and/or high frequency.

    Section 4: Conclusion

    • The conclusion should be one paragraph. Summarize what you have accomplished over the past year. You must also clearly discuss the most important lessons learned through the tape-out experience! Be specific!
